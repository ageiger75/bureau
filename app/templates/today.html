How to read this screen

What the badges mean, what each channel is, and how the arithmetic is built.

Badges

  • SELL-IN

    Invoiced to a partner, which is when the accounts recognise it — not when a shopper buys. How much of it reached a shopper, and when, is not measured by any source connected here. One month of it is as much a statement about a partner's ordering rhythm as about demand, which is why a shipment month is never a conversation with a market's lead.

  • CAUSE NOT MEASURED

    The gap is real and nothing connected here can say why. Either there is no funnel behind the figure — a partner's invoice has none — or the funnel exists and is not counted, as in stores without footfall counters. Not a fault to repair on the market's side, and not a reason to move the figure down the list.

  • NO COMMITMENT

    Nothing has been promised against this gap. Said once for the subject: printed on every channel it stopped being read, which is the opposite of what an absence is for.

The arithmetic

  • Driver impacts come from a chained decomposition: each driver is moved to its baseline value in turn, and the change in sales is attributed to it. They are estimates of contribution, never measured causes, and they sum exactly to the movement against the baseline named on the card.

  • The plan is the monthly planning workbook and nothing else. The warehouse also publishes a goals fact; it has no target at all for two fifths of what the group sells, and the business does not stand behind it.

  • The perimeter of the figure at the top is the published file's own: every channel, hospitality and corporate gifts included. The cards below cover what the warehouse reads, which is not quite the same set — the gap is closed by one source and explained by the other, and neither is asked to do the other's work.
